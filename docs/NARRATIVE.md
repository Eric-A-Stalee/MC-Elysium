# Writing and discovery

Elysium should be understandable to operate and incomplete to explain. A smith
can describe the order of the hammer strokes without predicting what that work
will do to the world. Do not turn practical instructions into warnings about
the coming plot.

Write records from the knowledge of the person who left them. Workshop accounts,
inspection notes and ordinary concerns belong beside incomplete or conflicting
observations. Prometheus remembers particular visitors and knows the craft; he
does not recite the player's future quest. Uncertainty should come from what the
writer could not establish, rather than repeated ominous slogans or evasive
riddles. Retain the mythic names and the strangeness of the place.

Give a clue where its source can be encountered. Instructions at a restraint
can describe servicing that restraint. A book found much earlier should not
list every later encounter, its solution, the opening of the vessel, or the
conditions for restoring the sky. Descriptions must not promise an interactive
mechanic that does not exist.

The optional `/elysium story` journal uses personal discoveries. Another player's
progression can change the realm without giving a new visitor knowledge of every
system. Recipe bills and controls remain explicit. The rear regulator explains
its choices once its fittings have been repaired.

Later advancements are hidden until earned. The first sunset, night and opened
vessel do not send toast notifications. Unexpected clock changes do not broadcast
chapter titles into the underground workshop. Sky records require exposure to the
sky; the vessel record requires examining the open vessel. Local sounds, smoke,
the actual sky and the encountered creatures carry those discoveries.

All authored book prose now lives in `assets/elysium/lang/en_us.json`. Keep each
page within Minecraft's 14 visible lines; the actual-client book probe checks all
books, including generated recipe pages. Do not pin a test to a particular poetic
ending. Developer route guides remain explicit references and contain spoilers.
